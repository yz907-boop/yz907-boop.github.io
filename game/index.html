Finless Porpoise Facts

About Finless Porpoises

  • Finless porpoises are small toothed whales that live in the Yangtze River.
  • They are called "finless" because they don't have a dorsal fin like other dolphins.
  • Instead, they have a low ridge covered in thick skin with tiny bumps.
  • They can grow up to 2.27 meters (7.4 feet) long and weigh up to 72 kg (159 pounds).
  • They are sometimes called "river pigs" in Chinese (江豚 - jiāng tún).

What They Eat

  • Finless porpoises are carnivores and eat mainly fish.
  • They also eat shrimp and other small river creatures.
  • They use echolocation to find their food in the murky river water.
  • Overfishing in the Yangtze River reduces their food supply.

Threats They Face

  • Pollution: Chemicals and plastic waste in the river harm porpoises.
  • Overfishing: Reduces their food supply and they can get caught in nets.
  • Boat Traffic: Collisions with ships and underwater noise pollution.
  • Dams: Block migration routes and change river conditions.
  • Habitat Loss: Development along the river destroys their living spaces.

How We Can Help

  • Support laws that protect the Yangtze River and its wildlife.
  • Reduce plastic use to prevent pollution in rivers and oceans.
  • Support sustainable fishing practices.
  • Learn more about finless porpoises and share what you know.
  • Donate to conservation organizations working to protect them.

Did you know?

The Yangtze finless porpoise is critically endangered with only about 1,000 individuals remaining in the wild. Conservation efforts have helped stabilize their population in recent years.

Conservation Research Highlights

Current Status

  • Less than 1,200 Yangtze finless porpoises remain in the wild
  • Current population is only half of what it was in the 1990s
  • 60% of the Yangtze's natural floodplains have been degraded or lost
  • Listed as critically endangered on the IUCN Red List

Major Threats

  • Habitat degradation from dams and river regulation
  • Water pollution from industrial and agricultural runoff
  • High-density shipping causing noise pollution and collisions
  • Overfishing reducing food supply
  • Extreme drought affecting water levels and prey availability

Conservation Efforts

  • 10-year fishing ban in the Yangtze River
  • Protected areas and nature reserves
  • Yangtze finless porpoises rescue action plan (2026-2035)
  • Artificial breeding and reintroduction programs
  • Monitoring and research initiatives

How You Can Help

  • Reduce plastic use to prevent pollution
  • Support sustainable fishing practices
  • Learn about and spread awareness of the species
  • Support organizations working on Yangtze conservation
  • Advocate for policies that protect freshwater ecosystems
